Content

Bharatpur Beach sits on the eastern edge of Neil Island, a five-minute walk from the jetty, and it is the only beach in the Andamans where you can step off a ferry and be snorkelling within the hour. The water is shallow and unusually clear over a live coral shelf, which is why the glass-bottom boats work here and nowhere else on the island.

It is also the busiest stretch on Neil, and that matters: the boats, the jet skis and the day-trippers all share the same 500 metres of sand. Come before ten in the morning and it is a different beach entirely.

Where it is and how to reach

Bharatpur is on Neil Island (Shaheed Dweep), roughly 40 minutes by private catamaran from Havelock and about two hours from Port Blair.

  • From Port Blair: ferry to Neil, roughly 2 hours on a private catamaran.
  • From Havelock: ferry to Neil, about 40 minutes, two or three sailings a day.
  • On the island: autos wait at the jetty; a scooter costs about ₹500 for the day.

What the beach is like

The sand is white and coarse, the slope is gentle and the water stays waist-deep a long way out. For a long swim, walk fifteen minutes to Laxmanpur.

Note: One afternoon on Neil? Spend the morning at Bharatpur and the sunset at Laxmanpur. Doing it the other way round puts you in the sun at the wrong end of the day.

Things to do

Bharatpur is the activity beach on Neil, and every operator works from the same strip of sand. Prices are broadly fixed but always confirm before you get in the boat.

Best time to visit

October to May, and within that, February and March give you the clearest water. The beach faces east, so it is at its best in the first three hours after sunrise.

Caption: The coral shelf sits close to the surface, which is why glass-bottom boats work here.

Facilities and what to carry

  1. Changing rooms and lockers near the entrance — a small fee, cash only.
  2. Two or three shacks selling water, tea and fried snacks.
  3. No shade beyond the tree line, so carry sunscreen.
  4. Reef shoes are worth it — the coral shelf is sharp underfoot in places.

Note: Please do not stand on the coral. The shelf here is alive and it is the reason the beach is worth visiting.

Nearby

Laxmanpur Beach is fifteen minutes west and is where the island watches the sunset. Sitapur Beach, on the eastern tip, is the sunrise counterpart and the quietest of the three.

Frequently asked questions

Question

Is Bharatpur Beach good for swimming?

answer

For wading and floating, yes — the water is shallow and calm. For a proper swim, no: the coral shelf starts a few metres out. Laxmanpur is the better swimming beach.

Question

Is there an entry fee?

answer

No. The beach is free to enter. You pay only for activities, lockers and parking.

Question

How long should I spend here?

answer

Two to three hours covers a glass-bottom boat ride, a snorkel and time on the sand.
