Content

Getting between the islands is the one part of an Andaman trip that people leave to the last minute, and it is the one part that punishes them for it. Ferries are the only practical link between Port Blair, Havelock and Neil, and on a busy week every good seat is gone days ahead.

Who runs the ferries

Two kinds of boats sail the same route, and they are not interchangeable. The private catamarans are faster, air-conditioned and sell out first. The government ferry is slower and much cheaper, but its schedule changes with the weather and tickets are sold from a counter, not online.

When to book

The honest answer is as soon as your flights are fixed. Between December and February the morning sailings fill two to three weeks ahead, and the afternoon ones are the first to be cancelled when the sea turns.

  • Peak season (Dec–Feb): book two to three weeks ahead.
  • Shoulder (Mar–May, Oct–Nov): a week is usually enough.
  • Monsoon (Jun–Sep): book late, and keep the day loose — sailings are cancelled at short notice.

The one mistake worth avoiding

Book the return sailing before the outward one. Travellers who book only the outward leg often find that the day they need to come back has nothing left, and a missed return ferry means a missed flight out of Port Blair. Work backwards from your flight home.

What to carry on the day

  • A printed ticket — phone signal at the jetty is unreliable.
  • The same photo ID whose number is on the ticket.
  • Motion-sickness tablets if you are prone to it; the crossing gets rough after March.

Common questions about Andaman ferries

Question

Can I book Andaman ferry tickets after I land?

answer

You can, but only for the government ferry, and only if seats are left. Private operators sell out days ahead in season and do not hold a counter quota for walk-ins.

Question

What happens if my ferry is cancelled?

answer

Private operators refund the fare or move you to the next available sailing. They do not cover a missed flight, which is why the return leg should always have a day of slack before you fly home.

Question

Is the government ferry worth taking to save money?

answer

On a long trip, yes. On a short one, no — the time you lose at the counter and on the slower crossing usually costs you half a day on the island you came to see.
